Order lifecycle

  1. An operator in the Operations Console, or a Scenario Controller run, sends POST /api/v1/orders to Order Service.
  2. Order Service stores the order and emits order.placed.v1 on box1.order.events.v1.
  3. Inventory Service reserves stock and answers with inventory.reserved.v1 or inventory.rejected.v1.
  4. Order Service moves the order forward and emits fulfillment.requested.v1.
  5. Fulfillment Adapter queues the request, writes an ERP1 file to the legacy ERP VM over SFTP, and reads back the result file.
  6. The adapter emits shipment.confirmed.v1 or shipment.failed.v1; Order and Inventory settle their state.
  7. Notification Service reacts to order and shipment events, emails the customer notice through MailHog over SMTP, and records notification.* events.

Equipment telemetry

  1. Site equipment sends POST /api/v1/telemetry/readings to Asset Service with a Keycloak bearer token (role telemetry-writer).
  2. Asset Service returns 202 once Kafka acknowledges equipment.reading.v1 on box1.telemetry.events.v1.
  3. Its own asset-service consumer group stores recent readings in PostgreSQL and raises equipment.threshold-exceeded.v1 alerts with hysteresis.
  4. The Operations Console reads /api/v1/alerts; Asset Service also serves its own telemetry console at /console.
  5. The contract reserves an ai-operations consumer group for Agentic Operations. That service is scaffolded with Kafka enabled but has no consumer yet.

Every service that owns data runs its own PostgreSQL in its own namespace (<service>-dev) and publishes through a transactional outbox. Kafka delivery is at least once; consumers record eventId with their business effect and key order topics by orderId.

“Contract only” means the group is listed in box1-ops-contracts/TOPICS.md but no service on main consumes it yet. The Operations Console authenticates operators with Keycloak (OIDC, PKCE) and calls the services over cluster-internal HTTP; Scenario Controller fetches a client-credentials token for its Inventory calls.
